# Manager × Team × Season Data Availability Matrix

Compiled 2026-08-10. Cross-references [`data/external/manager_tenures.md`](../../data/external/manager_tenures.md) for tenure ground truth. Two independent public sources were audited directly (downloaded and inspected, not assumed):

- **SOURCE A — FBref match-level advanced stats** (Opta-provided), mirrored as CSV release assets in the public `worldfootballR_data` GitHub repository (`fb_advanced_match_stats` release). Player × match granularity, 5 leagues (ENG/ESP/ITA/GER/FRA), verified coverage **Season_End_Year 2018–2026** (i.e. **2017/18 season onward**) in every league checked.
- **SOURCE B — StatsBomb Open Data.** Event-level + match metadata (crucially, **each team's manager per match is recorded in the source data itself** — `matches/{comp}/{season}.json` → `home_team.managers[]`/`away_team.managers[]`). Coverage is a curated, non-exhaustive set of competition-seasons (80 total as of audit date), not a full historical archive.

Both were downloaded and directly queried (not taken on faith from documentation) — see `src/data_sources.py` for the reproducible access layer.

**Observation with direct modelling consequences:** De Zerbi is the *only* one of the three managers whose in-scope sample spans multiple clubs and leagues within the FBref-covered window. Guardiola's and Klopp's in-scope FBref samples are each single-club. This is the opposite of what §18/§19 of the project brief (club-identity-bias risk) would prefer, and is a direct, unavoidable consequence of open-data coverage starting in 2017/18 — by which point Guardiola and Klopp had already settled into their long, single-club tenures. **Mitigations proposed** (see Phase 0 proposal, open decision OD-2): (a) supplement Guardiola with the StatsBomb Barcelona sample as a separate, non-pooled robustness check; (b) lean on the literature's own team-level validation (Plakias et al. 2025 independently cluster Barcelona, Bayern Munich *and* Manchester City together in one "structured positional play" style, and Liverpool with Atalanta in a "controlled possession + rapid counterattack" style — see `outputs/literature/literature_review_synthesis.md` §1.2) as external, non-circular evidence that these managers' signal is not purely club-specific, even though our own primary model cannot fully test this for Guardiola/Klopp within the open-data window; (c) be explicit and honest about this asymmetry as a stated limitation rather than hiding it.

## 3. Source comparability warning (feeds decision_log D-003)

FBref itself changed underlying data provider from **StatsBomb to Opta in late October 2022** (documented in the `worldfootballR_data` repository's own README for the legacy StatsBomb-sourced season file). The **match-level `fb_advanced_match_stats` release used here is uniformly Opta-derived across its entire 2017/18–2025/26 span** (verified: pressing/"pressures" columns are present in the season-level StatsBomb-era files but absent from the match-level files, confirming the match-level release never used the StatsBomb definitions). This means:
- The match-level dataset recommended above is **internally consistent** (one provider, one metric-definition set, throughout).
- It must **never** be pooled feature-for-feature with StatsBomb Open Data (Source B) without an explicit, documented harmonisation step. Where both are used (e.g. Guardiola's Barcelona via Source B vs. his Manchester City via Source A), they must be treated as two separate evidence streams, not concatenated rows of one dataset.
- Opta's match-level schema does **not** publish a direct "pressures" count analogous to StatsBomb's; pressing intensity must instead be approximated from tackles-by-pitch-third, interceptions, and recoveries (columns confirmed present: `Tkl_Tackles`, `Att 3rd_Tackles`/`Mid 3rd_Tackles`/`Def 3rd_Tackles`, `Int`, `Recov`). This is a genuine feature-engineering constraint, not an oversight — flagged for the Football Knowledge Layer design (see `football_ontology.md`).

## 4. Historical-gap chase (Bayern, Dortmund, Palermo, Shakhtar) — outcome

Following an explicit request to pursue additional sources for the excluded tenures before accepting the scope limitation (see `outputs/PHASE0_DELIVERABLE.md` open decision OD-2), the following were investigated directly, not assumed:

1. **FBref's own historical depth.** Confirmed empirically: the `worldfootballR_data` season-level file's `Home_xG`/`Away_xG` columns are **100% null for Bundesliga matches in the 2013/14–2015/16 window** (Guardiola's Bayern era) — even basic expected-goals data, let alone passing/pressing detail, does not exist on FBref for this period. This corroborates the known rollout timeline (FBref/Opta advanced statistics were introduced progressively from the 2017/18 season onward) and confirms the 2018-season-start cutoff found throughout Source A is a genuine data-non-existence wall, not a scraping or mirror-completeness artefact. A direct WebFetch of FBref's Palermo-2016/17 and Bayern-2015/16 squad-stats pages independently returned HTTP 403 (Cloudflare), consistent with the earlier finding that FBref cannot be fetched live from this environment at all.
2. **`worldfootballR_data`'s `match_results` release** (basic results, 47 countries including Germany and Ukraine, back to 1989) was checked directly — confirmed to contain only scores/dates/venue/referee (21 columns, no passing/tackle/possession detail), consistent with point 1 above.
3. **Kaggle "European Soccer Database" (Hugo Mathien)**, which does cover 2008–2016 (i.e. spans both Guardiola's Bayern and Klopp's whole Dortmund tenure), was located and its schema independently corroborated across three sources (a ResearchGate paper analysing it, its Kaggle/HuggingFace documentation): **7 tables — Country, League, Match, Player, Player_Attributes, Team, Team_Attributes.** Player/Team "attributes" are **EA Sports FIFA video-game ratings**, not measured performance data. The Match table's "detailed events" are XML blobs of **goals, shots on/off target, fouls, cards, crosses, corners and possession%** — i.e. box-score-level counting stats, not the passing-network/pressing/zone-occupation detail the football ontology (`football_ontology.md`) is built around. Even if fully retrieved (a ~313MB SQLite file, mirrored without authentication on Hugging Face at `julien-c/kaggle-hugomathien-soccer`; download from this environment proved too slow/unreliable to complete, ~90KB/s), it would only support crude proxies (shot volume, cross volume, foul/card counts, coarse possession%) for Bayern/Dortmund — and per decision D-003, could not be pooled with the Opta-derived primary dataset regardless.
4. **Ukrainian Premier League / Shakhtar 2021/22** and **Palermo 2016/17**: no open dataset with tactical event detail was found for either. The Ukrainian league is absent from StatsBomb's 80 covered competition-seasons entirely, and the season is independently flagged CAUTION in the tenure table for being abandoned/war-disrupted regardless of data availability.

**Conclusion: the chase is genuinely exhausted, not merely deprioritised.** No source was found, in the time available, that provides passing/pressing/possession-zone-level detail for Bayern Munich (2013–16), Borussia Dortmund (2008–15), Palermo (2016/17), or Shakhtar Donetsk (2021/22) comparable in kind to the primary Opta match-level dataset. The one concrete improvement from this exercise is the **Benevento 2017/18 correction above** (§1), which was missed in the first audit pass. The original scope recommendation (Man City / Liverpool / Sassuolo+Brighton+Marseille, +Benevento as a flagged low-confidence addition) stands, now on the basis of a documented, exhausted search rather than an assumption.

## 5. Column-level coverage audit — a critical gap found during Task #4, not Task #2

The audits in §1-4 above check whether **rows/matches** exist for a team-season (they do, reliably, for all in-scope clubs). They do **not** check whether the **specific columns** a given feature needs actually have non-null values within those rows — and it turns out several do not, for a subset of leagues and seasons. This was missed in the original Task #2 integrity audit and only surfaced when the Task #4 club-identity-bias check produced a suspiciously clean two-cluster split (Sassuolo+Benevento vs. everyone else, correlation ≈ -0.6) that turned out to be a data-coverage artefact rather than a real finding — see `decision_log.md` D-006 and `outputs/results/task4_evaluation_findings.md` for the full incident.

Direct per-column, per-league, per-season non-null-rate check (one representative column per stat block):

| stat block | ENG | ITA | FRA |
|---|---|---|---|
| `defense` (e.g. `Tkl_Tackles`) | 100% always | 100% always | 100% always |
| `misc` (e.g. `Recov`) | 100% always | 100% always | 100% always |
| `passing` (e.g. `PrgP`) | 100% always | **0% for 2018-2023, 100% from 2024** | **0% for 2018-2023, 100% from 2024** |
| `passing_types` | 100% always | **0% for 2018-2023, 100% from 2024** | **0% for 2018-2023, 100% from 2024** |
| `possession` (e.g. `Touches_Touches`) | 100% always | **0% for 2018-2023, 100% from 2024** | **0% for 2018-2023, 100% from 2024** |
| `keeper` | ~99-100% always | **0% for 2018-2023, 100% from 2024** | **0% for 2018-2023, 100% from 2024** |
| `summary` (e.g. `Touches`, `Sh`, `SoT`, `SCA_SCA`) | **0% before 2025, 100% from 2025** | same | same |

**Consequence for our in-scope sample:** Sassuolo (2018/19-2020/21, 3 full seasons) and Benevento (2017/18) — both De Zerbi tenures — have **zero data** for anything requiring `passing`/`passing_types`/`possession`/`keeper`. Brighton (2022/23-2023/24, ENG) and Marseille (2024/25 onward, FRA, inside the post-2024 window) are unaffected. `summary`'s complete absence before 2025 affects **every** club/manager equally, and required redesigning two of the seven football concepts (`chance_creation`, `box_presence`) to drop the terms that depended on it — see `decision_log.md` D-006 for exactly what changed.

**Why this wasn't caught in Task #2:** the Task #2 integrity audit (`outputs/audit/integrity_audit.md`) checked match counts, player counts and minutes — all of which were correct, because the *rows* exist (a Sassuolo player's row is present in the passing table for every 2018/19 match, just with null values in every stat column). The lesson for any future notebook in this project: **row presence is not the same as value presence** — always spot-check non-null rates on the specific columns a feature will use, not just row/match counts, before trusting a new data source.

## 6. What was ruled out, and why (per §17 of the brief)

- **FBref direct scraping** — blocked (HTTP 403, Cloudflare bot-protection) from this environment; no local Chrome/Chromium available to bypass via `soccerdata`'s browser-automation path. Using the pre-scraped `worldfootballR_data` release assets instead avoids scraping entirely and is more reproducible (fixed, versioned CSV files rather than a live, blockable scrape).
- **Understat** (via `soccerdata`) — accessible and fast, but schema is attacking-only (19 columns: goals, xG, assists, xA, shots, key passes — no passing detail, no defensive/pressing metrics). Insufficient alone for a multi-dimensional tactical ontology; not pursued further as a primary source, though it remains available as a cross-check for the attacking/chance-creation dimension specifically if needed later.
- **StatsBomb Open Data as the sole/primary source** — ruled out as primary because coverage of the three managers is too sparse outside Guardiola's Barcelona (Klopp: 33 team-matches total across his whole career in the open dataset; De Zerbi: **zero** matches in StatsBomb Open Data — none of his managed teams/seasons appear in the 80 available competition-seasons at all). Retained as a valuable *supplementary* source specifically for Guardiola's Barcelona era, and as the source of ground-truth match-level manager identity used to sanity-check the tenure table (see `src/data_sources.py::sb_match_manager_index`).
