# Manager Playing-Philosophy Profiles: Evidence-Based Hypotheses

**Status:** DRAFT — all three manager sections complete and sourced. Klopp and De Zerbi sections are based on a lighter-touch direct search than Guardiola's (which was produced by a since-terminated research agent with a much larger source budget); one explicit evidence gap is flagged (De Zerbi's out-of-possession/pressing profile, §3.6/§4.4-6) and should be closed with follow-up research before the manager profiles are finalised for modelling. Compiled 2026-08-10.

**Method note.** Claims are labelled by evidence type throughout:
- **[data-confirmed]** — a named statistic from a specific provider (Opta/StatsBomb), attributed to a fetched source that states it.
- **[observed]** — qualitative tactical analysis (video-based), attributed to a named analyst/outlet.
- **[quote]** — the manager's own words, <15 words, quotation-marked, attributed.
- **[analyst consensus, uncited]** — widely-held analytical view for which no single citable source was found. Used sparingly and flagged explicitly.

Never treat [analyst consensus, uncited] as equivalent evidentiary weight to [data-confirmed]. This distinction feeds directly into the "three types of evidence" requirement for the final compatibility explanations (data-derived evidence / football interpretation / managerial relevance).

## 1. Pep Guardiola

### 1.1 Core principles (his own words)
- "Football is about rationally occupying the space, and the ball goes to the space." **[quote]** — 2007 coaching clinic, transcribed by David Garcia (It's Just a Sport).
- "The intention is not to move the ball, but move the opponent." **[quote]** — same source.
- "I loathe all that passing for the sake of it, all that tiki-taka." **[quote]** — *Pep Confidential* (Martí Perarnau), quoted to Bayern players.
- "Modern football is not positional." **[quote]** — Sky Sports, Oct 2020 context; Opta Analyst, 2025, naming Bournemouth/Newcastle/Brighton/Liverpool as "modern football" by contrast.

**Synthesised principle [observed]:** possession is instrumental, not terminal — circulation exists to displace opponents and manufacture superiorities (numerical, positional, qualitative), not to retain the ball as an end. Perarnau Magazine frames a "totality" principle: attacking and defending shape are one continuous structure.

### 1.2 Build-up / first phase
- Goalkeeper is a build-up player: short from goal-kicks and open play, "from both goal-kicks and open play", to manufacture an extra passing option and force a press/concede-territory dilemma **[observed]** (Coaches' Voice, 2021).
- Explicit adaptation rule: vs a two-forward press → back three; vs three forwards → keep four defenders **[observed]** (Coaches' Voice, 2021).
- Both single-pivot (2-3) and double-pivot (3-2) build-up shapes used across his City tenure, with the double pivot's specific purpose being to "manipulate the opponent's second line" **[observed]** (Spielverlagerung, 2017). Mature version (2026): single pivot with staggered heights ("4-2" low base: GK + 3 CB + 2 DM) **[observed]** (Total Football Analysis, 2026).
- Deliberate pause on the ball to bait the press ("la pausa") **[observed]** (Perarnau Magazine).

### 1.3 Possession model — *juego de posición*
- Pitch divided into 4 vertical lines / 5 vertical strips (2 flanks, 2 half-spaces, 1 centre) **[observed]**.
- Hard positioning rule, triple-sourced: **max 3 players on any horizontal line, max 2 on any vertical line**, to guarantee triangles/diamonds and 2-3 passing options for the ball-carrier **[observed]** (Perarnau Magazine; Coaches' Voice; Marcus DiBernardo's 20-cell grid analysis).
- Guardiola reportedly targets 15+ completed passes to fully structure an attacking phase and pre-position players for the counter-press that follows a turnover **[observed]** (Perarnau Magazine) — i.e. circulation volume is a *defensive* pre-condition, not an end in itself. This resolves the apparent tension with his anti-tiki-taka quote.
- Origin: Rinus Michels/Cruyff lineage at Ajax/Barcelona **[observed]**.

### 1.4 Progression mechanisms
- Third-man combinations: deep pass → checking player lays off → third player advances **[observed]**, explicitly for risk management (fewer players committed forward, deeper protection against the counter).
- Half-space passing to a "free" wide central midfielder who can dribble into a 3v2 **[observed]** (Spielverlagerung, 2018).
- Pinning: strikers occupy opposition centre-backs, an attacking midfielder pins the opposition pivot(s), mathematically freeing a midfielder elsewhere **[observed]**.
- Ball-far full-back is characteristically the free man for switches, producing a 2v1 out wide **[observed]** (Total Football Analysis, 2026).
- Final-third entry priority order **[observed]**: (1) central combination through the box midfield, (2) wide switch via a dropping/pinning attacking midfielder, (3) direct long ball to the striker in a 3v3 (used specifically against man-marking opponents).

### 1.5 Chance creation
- **[data-confirmed]** 2018/19: 36 shots from inside the 6-yard box (league-most), xG 63.06 (league-most) (ESPN/Opta, Michael Cox, 2019).
- **[observed]** Creation methods: stretch defence in width+depth; patient build-up into wide through-balls; low driven cut-backs from narrow positions rather than traditional touchline crosses; far-post runs isolating the striker against the CB pair.
- **[data-confirmed]** 2022/23: Haaland received 57 "big chances" — double any previous league leader (Opta, via premierleague.com/Coaches' Voice).
- **[observed, model-derived]** Flank asymmetry (right: in-to-out funnel/overloads/half-space crossing; left: underlapping midfield runs) nonetheless produces a broadly symmetrical expected-threat map — Analytics FC's own framing, explicitly xT-based rather than raw-counted.

### 1.6 Out-of-possession model
- High defensive line, explicit rationale: **[quote]** "The high line has always been like this for me" (Sky Sports, Oct 2020); belief that conceding fewest requires playing ~40m from goal.
- Preferred block: 4-1-4-1 → 4-4-2 when defending longer spells; wide forwards press inward onto centre-backs, striker screens the opposition pivot **[observed]** (Coaches' Voice, 2021).
- Barcelona-era recovery heuristic: if the ball isn't won back within ~5-6 seconds of a turnover, drop into the block rather than continue chasing **[observed]** (Perarnau Magazine).
- Counter-press *type* matters and evolved: Barcelona's was **lane/interception-oriented** (block passing lanes, bait a pass, intercept); Bayern's was more **direct-challenge-oriented**; City blended both **[observed]** (Coaches' Voice Coach Watch).
- Rest defence: favours a **3-2** shape beneath the front five (vs Klopp's 2-3) **[observed]** (Coaches' Voice, "What is rest defence?", 2024).

### 1.7 Transition behaviour
- **[data-confirmed]** After John Stones' hybrid CB/DM role was introduced: direct attacks conceded 1.2 → 0.6/game; opposition ball-progression speed 1.47 → 1.18 m/s (slowest in the league); opposition progression distance 10.9m → 9.8m (lowest in league); goals conceded 0.9 → 0.5/game (Opta Analyst, Raman, 2023). This is the strongest quantitative evidence in the whole file that his structural innovations are transition-suppression devices, not only attacking ones.
- Escape hierarchy on winning the ball: long release to a forward/winger first; safe short combination only if no long option exists **[observed]** (Perarnau Magazine).

### 1.8 Signature/idiosyncratic mechanisms and their evolution
- **Inverted/false full-back**: Bayern (Lahm/Alaba, to free wingers for 1v1s) → City (Delph/Zinchenko/Cancelo, to build a 3-2-2-3 with a midfield overload) → dissolved further into "everted midfielders" (Rico Lewis, O'Reilly) and centre-back conversions (Aké, Gvardiol) as auxiliary full-backs, by 2024/25 down to ~2 overlapping/underlapping runs per game, second-fewest in the league **[data-confirmed]**, Opta Analyst 2025.
- **Box midfield / double pivot**: creates a 7v6 against a 4-3-3 **[observed]**; Guardiola's own rationale for inverted full-backs, in his words: **[quote]** "I like to play with wingers high and wide" and **[quote]** "I am a big fan of short passes" (Sky Sports exclusive, Adam Bate, 2023).
- **False 9**: no natural striker 2020/21-2021/22 (De Bruyne/Foden/Gündogan rotating), 13/38 matches in 2020/21 **[data-confirmed]**; superseded by Haaland as a direct target man from 2022/23 — a genuine reversal of the possession-purist model in response to more physical opposition.
- **Goalkeeper as an auxiliary passer**: Hart → Bravo → Ederson, explicitly for distribution; Ederson holds 7 PL assists, most by any goalkeeper in league history **[data-confirmed]** (Opta Analyst, 2025). By 2025/26 the signing of Donnarumma (an elite shot-stopper, comparatively risk-averse in distribution) suggests this specific signature may be softening.

### 1.9 What is stable vs club/squad-contingent
**Stable across Barcelona → Bayern → City (the manager signal):**
- The positional grid rule (max 3 horizontal / 2 vertical)
- Circulation-as-displacement rather than circulation-as-retention
- Deliberate baiting of the press
- High defensive line rationale
- Goalkeeper/back-line as first-phase passers

**Club/squad-contingent (confounds to control for):**
- Exact full-back inversion mechanism (personnel-dependent: Lahm/Alaba vs Delph/Zinchenko/Cancelo vs Lewis/O'Reilly)
- False-9 vs target-man (Haaland) — a major, data-visible reversal driven by squad and opposition physicality, **not** a change in underlying principle
- Absolute possession volume — 71.9% at peak City (2017/18) vs a 2025/26 low of 32.8% in a single match **[data-confirmed]**, showing raw possession share is highly context-dependent even within one managerial tenure
- **[data-confirmed]** League-wide directness trend 2025/26 (passes/sequence 5.12→3.97, direct speed 1.43→1.61 m/s) shows even Guardiola's teams drift toward directness as the league adapts — a warning that "possession volume" alone will not cleanly separate him from the field in later seasons.

### 1.10 Modelling warnings specific to Guardiola
1. Barcelona 2008-12 and City 2016-2026 both featured elite, expensively-assembled squads; raw passing/possession volume partly reflects **squad quality**, not only philosophy. Positional/structural features (line occupancy, pass-network shape) are more likely to isolate the manager signal than volume features.
2. Vilanova (Barcelona 2012/13) and Maresca (City 2026/27) are **stylistic continuations** — do not use immediately-following seasons at these clubs as a "not-Guardiola" control.
3. The false-9 → Haaland reversal means "striker aerial/target-man" features will *misclassify* Guardiola-era players if the model is trained predominantly on the false-9 seasons, or vice versa. Consider treating this as an intra-manager regime split if time permits, rather than assuming one Guardiola profile is stationary over ten City seasons.
4. Bayern's undersized centre-backs (Kimmich/Alaba) behind a high line succeeded partly due to individual quality — high defensive line height is a stable principle, but its statistical footprint (goals conceded, opposition through-balls) is squad-dependent.

## 2. Jürgen Klopp

### 2.1 Core principles
- "He likes having the ball, playing football, passes. It's like an orchestra. But it's a silent song. I like heavy metal more. I always want it loud." **[quote]** — Klopp contrasting his approach with Wenger's (Coaches' Voice).
- Gegenpressing's stated purpose in his own framing: **[observed, near-quote]** "Gegenpressing lets you win back the ball nearer to the goal" — i.e. immediate re-pressing after a turnover, rather than retreating to regroup, so the next attack starts from a higher, more dangerous position (Goal.com; Total Football Analysis).

**Synthesised principle [observed]:** for Klopp, defending and attacking are the same act performed in reverse — the team that wins the ball back fastest, closest to the opponent's goal, gets the highest-value transition. Possession itself is not prized; the *moments immediately after a turnover* (in either direction) are where his teams are designed to hurt opponents most.

### 2.2 Build-up / first phase
- Early Liverpool (2015-17): 4-2-3-1 shifting to 4-3-3, with an explicit emphasis on transition defending — "aggressive counter-pressing midfield unit" (Emre Can, Milner, Lallana) plus Firmino's "back-pressing" as auxiliary screening **[observed]** (Coaches' Voice).
- **[data-confirmed]** 2015/16 (Klopp's partial first season): Liverpool were the Premier League's highest-ranked team for defensive duels, and rose from 14th to 2nd-highest scorers over the 29 matches following his arrival (Coaches' Voice, citing performance data).
- Later Liverpool (2023/24): Trent Alexander-Arnold inverted into a build-up midfield role, producing 3-2 or 4-2 first-phase shapes — a build-up structure that converges, structurally, with the "extra central passer" logic seen in both Guardiola's and De Zerbi's systems, though arrived at from a different starting principle (transition efficiency rather than positional occupation) **[observed]**.

### 2.3 Possession model
- Klopp's possession is explicitly **not** an end state: circulation is tolerated only insofar as it sets up quick vertical progression. This is the sharpest conceptual contrast with Guardiola's "totality"/15-pass structuring principle, even though both ultimately aim to manufacture transition opportunities **[observed, synthesised]**.
- Early-Liverpool front three (Salah/Mané/Firmino) combined individual 1v1 ability with rotational movement, Firmino dropping to create central overloads and open space for late full-back arrivals **[observed]**.

### 2.4 Progression mechanisms
- **[data-confirmed]** In every season 2018-2023, Liverpool recorded at least the second-most crosses in the Premier League; Alexander-Arnold and Robertson ranked joint-3rd and 5th for assists in 2018/19, then 2nd and 3rd (behind only De Bruyne) in the title-winning 2019/20 season (Coaches' Voice).
- Primary progression route (2018-2023 era): overlapping/underlapping full-backs delivering crosses from advanced wide positions, fed by a front three creating central disorder **[observed]**.
- **[data-confirmed]** 2023/24: crossing volume fell to its lowest since 2018, and Alexander-Arnold's crosses-per-90 hit their lowest since 2017, replaced by central underlap/overload patterns and Núñez's central runs in behind — a shift toward more central, less wide-cross-dependent progression **[observed]**.

### 2.5 Chance creation
- Early-to-mid era: wide crossing volume from advanced full-backs is the dominant, statistically visible creation mechanism **[data-confirmed]** (crossing rank data above).
- Later era: central combination play and direct runs in behind (Núñez) supersede crossing as the primary creation route **[observed]**. This is an important within-manager evolution: a model trained only on 2018-2020 Liverpool would over-weight "crossing volume" as a Klopp signature, which the 2023/24 data would then contradict.

### 2.6 Out-of-possession model — pressing and counter-pressing
- **Gegenpressing** ("counter-pressing"): on losing the ball, the team presses immediately and with numbers, particularly when possession is lost in the opponent's half, aiming to force an error in the opponent's own build-up rather than retreat and regroup **[observed]** (Goal.com; Total Football Analysis; consistent across all fetched sources — this is the least contested claim in the corpus).
- This is functionally the mirror image of counter-pressing as practised by Guardiola (which is lane/interception-oriented and follows a slow, structured possession phase) — Klopp's press is trigger-based off a *turnover*, arriving from a higher-tempo, lower-structure possession phase **[observed, synthesised]**.

### 2.7 Transition behaviour
- Central to the whole model: the team is explicitly organised to maximise the value of the moments immediately after regaining possession, attacking "at high tempo" into the space between the opponent's centre-backs and full-backs **[observed]**.
- Chance creation, defensive organisation and pressing are treated as three faces of one transition-centred plan, rather than three separate phases — this is the single most load-bearing distinction from both Guardiola and De Zerbi, whose models both foreground structured possession as the primary organising principle.

### 2.8 Signature/idiosyncratic mechanisms and evolution
- **Full-back-as-primary-creator** (Alexander-Arnold, Robertson, 2018-2023) → **full-back-as-auxiliary-midfielder in an inverted build-up role** (Alexander-Arnold, 2023/24) **[data-confirmed for both]**. This is a direct, verified within-Klopp evolution and should be treated as an internal regime split for modelling purposes, analogous to Guardiola's false-9 → Haaland reversal.
- Front-three rotational pressing/dropping (Firmino's "false 9-as-presser" role) → central striker making direct runs in behind (Núñez) **[observed]**.

### 2.9 Modelling warnings specific to Klopp
1. Crossing volume is a strong Klopp signature **only** for the 2018-2023 Liverpool window; using it as a stationary feature across his whole Liverpool tenure (or projecting it onto Dortmund/Mainz) risks contradiction by the later data **[data-confirmed evolution above]**.
2. "Gegenpressing intensity" is the dimension most likely to discriminate Klopp cleanly from Guardiola and De Zerbi (all sources agree it is his most stable, most quoted signature) — but it is exactly the dimension hardest to measure reliably from player-season aggregate statistics without possession-adjustment, since a team that presses well concedes fewer opposition possessions to press against in absolute terms.
3. Early Liverpool (2015/16) is split mid-season with Brendan Rodgers (see tenure table K3) — do not use 2015/16 aggregate Liverpool statistics without the match-level split already flagged as UNSAFE in the tenure table.

## 3. Roberto De Zerbi

### 3.1 Core principles
- No direct De Zerbi quote on his own philosophy was found in the sources fetched for this file; the clearest third-party endorsement is Klopp's own: "I told De Zerbi to keep turning the football world upside down. I will watch it from some distance. I respect so much what he's doing." **[quote, but from Klopp about De Zerbi, not De Zerbi himself]** (Coaches' Voice).
- **Synthesised principle [observed]:** De Zerbi's signature contribution is inverting the pressing/possession relationship that both Guardiola and Klopp otherwise share as a common assumption (that pressing is primarily something done *to* an opponent, out of possession). De Zerbi instead treats his *own* build-up as the mechanism that manufactures turnovers: rather than pressing to win the ball back after losing it, his teams deliberately construct situations, while *in* possession, that invite the opponent to press into a trap, so that winning the ball back happens as a by-product of his own team's shape, not a defensive reaction (SoccerTutor.com; Medium/SHfootball).

### 3.2 Build-up / first phase — the signature mechanism
- The goalkeeper functions as an auxiliary third centre-back, splitting the two central defenders in deep build-up **[observed]** (SoccerTutor.com; Coaches' Voice).
- Centre-backs "hold the ball, kill it dead, and invite the first presser" — i.e. a deliberate pause on the ball specifically designed to draw an opponent out of position **[observed]** (SoccerTutor.com). This is structurally similar to Guardiola's "la pausa" but is used more aggressively and more often as the primary attacking mechanism rather than an occasional device.
- Base deep-build-up structure: a 4-2 block behind the ball (back four + double pivot), with the goalkeeper making it a seven-player unit — deliberately one more passing option than most opponents' first press line can mark **[observed]** (SoccerTutor.com; Coaches' Voice).
- If the opposition commits to the press, the extra/free man appears via a dropping pivot, a number 10 between the lines, or the goalkeeper; if the opponent over-commits, a clipped pass goes over the top into the vacated space **[observed]** (SoccerTutor.com).

### 3.3 Possession model
- **[data-confirmed]** Sassuolo 2020/21: highest possession share in Serie A. Shakhtar 2021/22: top for possession in the league (Coaches' Voice).
- **[observed]** Typical season possession >55%, spiking into the high-60s/low-70s in individual matches (multiple tactical-analysis sources, consistent figures though not independently corroborated to a single primary dataset here — flag as observed rather than data-confirmed pending direct verification against the project's own StatsBomb/FBref data).
- Possession is treated as the *mechanism* by which pressure is manufactured and exploited, not primarily as territorial control — the clearest conceptual overlap with Guardiola, and the reason the two managers are expected to be difficult to separate on raw possession-share features (see §4.3 below).

### 3.4 Progression mechanisms
- Reversal patterns: after drawing the opposition's press with short combinations, the ball is played back into a centre-forward or a false-10 figure who has dropped into the vacated central space, or clipped directly over the top of the committed press **[observed]** (Coaches' Voice).
- Marseille (2024-2026): alternates between 3-2-5 and 3-1-6 attacking structures, aimed at maximising rational space occupation and numerical overloads against the opponent's back line **[observed]** (Total Football Analysis, 2026) — structurally closer to Guardiola's positional grid vocabulary than to anything in the Klopp corpus.

### 3.5 Chance creation
- De Zerbi's own coined mechanism, per analyst framing: "possessional transition" — generating counter-attack-like penetration *while already in possession*, rather than via a defensive turnover, by using the press-baiting build-up to create disorganised opposition shape further up the pitch **[observed]** (Yahoo Sports/analyst synthesis). This is the single most distinctive De Zerbi concept in the corpus and the best candidate "signature dimension" for the model to try to detect.

### 3.6 Out-of-possession model
- Double pivot provides defensive screening in front of the back four, with one pivot man-tracking dropping opposition midfielders **[observed]** (Coaches' Voice).
- No source fetched for this file gives De Zerbi's pressing height/block shape out of possession with the same specificity as the build-up mechanism — this is a genuine gap in the current research and should be revisited with a dedicated search before finalising the manager profile (flagged, not fabricated).

### 3.7 Evolution across clubs
- Sassuolo (2018-2021): 4-3-3 initially (2018/19), then converging toward the mature build-up structure; **[data-confirmed]** highest Serie A possession share by 2020/21, but also, in the same window, the league's 7th-worst defensive record in 2019/20 and most headed goals conceded — i.e. the attractive, high-possession model coexisted with real defensive fragility at a mid-table club, exactly the "intent vs. resourced output" gap flagged in the tenure table (§1.10 point 3 applies here too, mutatis mutandis).
- Shakhtar (2021/22, abandoned season): possession dominance in a weak league, consistent with the tenure-table warning that this sample is inflated by opposition quality, not philosophy strength alone.
- Brighton (2022-2024): 4-2-3-1 as the base formation, refining the goalkeeper/CB build-up mechanism with better-resourced players than Sassuolo or Shakhtar.
- Marseille (2024-2026): evolution toward back-three-based 3-2-5/3-1-6 attacking structures — a formation change, but the underlying press-baiting build-up principle is reported as continuous **[observed]**.

### 3.8 Modelling warnings specific to De Zerbi
1. Sassuolo's coexistence of high possession with poor defensive results (7th-worst defence 2019/20 despite 8th-place finish) means possession/build-up features alone will not cleanly separate "De Zerbi the philosophy" from "a mid-table team that concedes a lot" — defensive-output features must be interpreted cautiously for this manager specifically.
2. Shakhtar's 2021/22 numbers are inflated by a weak, war-disrupted league context (see tenure table D6) and should be weighted down or excluded, not treated as equally informative as the Sassuolo or Brighton samples.
3. The Palermo (D3) and Benevento (D4) tenures are both short, mid-season, and confounded by historically bad squads (Benevento: worst start in Serie A history under his predecessor) — these should likely be excluded from the manager-profile-construction sample entirely rather than down-weighted, per the tenure table's own recommendation.
4. Out-of-possession pressing height/detail is currently the weakest-evidenced dimension of the De Zerbi profile in this file and should not be treated as equally well-established as the build-up mechanism.

### 4.2 Falsifiable hypotheses (grouped by dimension)

**Pressing / defensive intensity**
- H-P1 (*strongly expected*): Players under Klopp show higher pressing-intensity and pressures-per-defensive-action figures than players under Guardiola or De Zerbi at equivalent positions.
- H-P2 (*expected*): Defensive-line-height proxies (e.g. mean defensive-action distance from own goal) are higher for Guardiola's and Klopp's teams than for De Zerbi's, though the mechanism differs (Guardiola: structural high line; Klopp: aggressive press).
- H-P3 (*uncertain/contested*): De Zerbi's teams will NOT show elevated out-of-possession pressing-intensity statistics despite their attacking reputation, because his mechanism operates primarily in-possession — this is testable and, if confirmed, is a genuinely novel finding for the project to report.

**Build-up / possession**
- H-B1 (*strongly expected*): Goalkeeper pass-volume and goalkeeper-to-outfield short-pass share are elevated for both Guardiola's and De Zerbi's teams relative to Klopp's, and relative to the league.
- H-B2 (*expected*): Guardiola and De Zerbi will be difficult to separate on possession-share and short-passing-volume features alone (see §4.3).
- H-B3 (*expected*): Klopp's teams show lower possession share but higher direct-speed-of-attack metrics than either Guardiola's or De Zerbi's.

**Progression and chance creation**
- H-C1 (*expected*): Klopp's teams (2018-2023 window specifically) show elevated crossing volume relative to Guardiola's and De Zerbi's; this hypothesis should be tested **within season windows**, not across Klopp's whole tenure, given the documented 2023/24 shift away from crossing (§2.4).
- H-C2 (*expected*): Guardiola's teams show a higher share of shots from central/cut-back zones relative to wide-cross-assisted shots, compared to Klopp's teams.
- H-C3 (*uncertain/contested*): De Zerbi's teams show elevated "progressive-carry-into-final-third" figures for central midfielders/false-10 profiles, reflecting the drop-off-the-press mechanism — this is a specific, checkable prediction from §3.2/3.4 rather than a generic possession claim.

**Transitions**
- H-T1 (*strongly expected*): Klopp's teams show the highest ball-recovery speed and shortest time-to-shot after regaining possession in the final third, of the three managers.
- H-T2 (*uncertain/contested*): De Zerbi's teams show elevated "counter-attack-like" final-third entry speed metrics despite starting from settled possession (the "possessional transition" claim, §3.5) — if the data does not support this, it should be reported as a case where football-analyst narrative is not confirmed by the statistics, which is itself a valid and useful finding.

**Squad/role-contingent confounds (explicitly not manager signals)**
- H-X1: False-9 usage will be elevated for Guardiola's 2020-22 City sample and should NOT be treated as a stable Guardiola signature when pooled against his Bayern/Barcelona/2022-26 City samples (§1.9, §1.10).
- H-X2: Crossing volume will be elevated for Klopp's 2018-23 Liverpool sample and should NOT be treated as a stable Klopp signature when pooled against 2023/24 or Dortmund-era data (§2.9).

### 4.3 Where the three managers are genuinely similar (the hard-to-separate cases)
- **Guardiola vs. De Zerbi** are the closest pair on almost every build-up and possession-volume dimension: both build deliberately through the goalkeeper and centre-backs, both use possession instrumentally to manipulate opponent shape, and both can post similar aggregate possession-share and short-passing-volume numbers. The discriminating dimensions are expected to be (a) the *specific mechanism* — Guardiola's rigid positional grid (max 3 horizontal/2 vertical) vs. De Zerbi's press-baiting pause-and-invite — which is a structural/sequential feature, not a volume feature, and will require careful feature engineering (e.g. pass-sequence pattern features, not just counts) to detect; and (b) pressing intensity out of possession, where Guardiola is expected to score materially higher than De Zerbi (H-P1/H-P3).
- **Klopp vs. the other two** is expected to be the easiest pairwise separation, since pressing intensity and transition-speed are both strongly and consistently evidenced as his signature across every source in this file, and both dimensions are comparatively well-measured by standard event-data metrics (pressures, recoveries, direct-speed-of-attack).
- **Risk to flag honestly in the report:** if Guardiola and De Zerbi cannot be statistically separated by the model, this is not necessarily a modelling failure — it may be an accurate reflection of genuine tactical proximity between two managers from a shared possession-football lineage, and the project should be prepared to report this as a finding rather than force a separation that the data does not support (directly relevant to the club-identity-bias validation question in the project brief, §19 of the brief).

### 4.4 Warnings for the modelling (squad-strength / naive-statistics traps)
1. **Team-strength confound (Guardiola, City and Bayern; Klopp, Liverpool 2018-23):** both managers had access to elite, well-resourced squads for large parts of their sampled tenures. Raw volume statistics (passes completed, possession share, chances created) partly reflect squad quality and opponent weakness, not philosophy. Prefer *structural/positional* features (e.g. pass-network shape, zone-occupation ratios) over raw volume where possible, and consider possession-adjusting or opponent-adjusting defensive/pressing metrics.
2. **Team-weakness confound cutting the other way (De Zerbi, Sassuolo and Benevento; Klopp, early Mainz):** De Zerbi's Sassuolo were mid-table with real defensive fragility, and his Benevento tenure inherited a historically bad squad. His *intent* (documented qualitatively above) may be statistically invisible or even contradicted in the aggregate numbers from these clubs. The tenure table already flags Benevento and Palermo for likely exclusion; Sassuolo should be retained but interpreted cautiously, with defensive-output features down-weighted for this manager.
3. **Mid-season contamination:** every mid-season appointment/departure flagged UNSAFE in the tenure table (Klopp K1/K3, De Zerbi D1/D3/D4/D7/D8/D9) must be excluded or split at match level before any manager-profile aggregation — this is a hard data-integrity requirement, not a modelling nicety, restated here because several of these rows are also the seasons with the richest available data (e.g. 2015/16 Liverpool, 2022/23 Brighton).
4. **Within-manager regime shifts:** both Guardiola (false-9 → Haaland target-man) and Klopp (crossing-heavy → central-underlap) show a documented, data-confirmed change in a feature that might otherwise be treated as a stable "manager signature". Pooling all seasons under one manager profile risks averaging away exactly the mechanism the project is trying to detect. Recommend testing whether season-windowed or era-split manager profiles produce cleaner separation than one profile per manager pooled across all seasons — this is a genuine open methodological decision, not yet resolved by the literature review (which found no paper doing this at manager level) or by this research.
5. **"False control" seasons** (already flagged in the tenure table): Vilanova's Barcelona 2012/13 and Maresca's City 2026/27 are stylistic continuations of Guardiola, not independent comparisons; Potter's Brighton is a partial stylistic precursor to De Zerbi. Do not use these as "not-manager-X" negative examples in any classification setup.
6. **De Zerbi's out-of-possession profile is currently under-evidenced** in this file relative to Guardiola's and Klopp's (§3.6) — before finalising the manager profile, this specific gap should be closed with additional targeted research (pressing height, PPDA-equivalent behaviour, block shape) rather than left to the data alone to reveal, since a thin qualitative profile makes it harder to interpret whatever the model finds.
